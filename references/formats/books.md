# Books and long-form manuscripts

Identify the genre and the publisher's house style. A novel, a practical nonfiction book, and an essay collection need different pacing. Follow the language guide's punctuation reference for dialogue, quotations, asides, and sentence endings.

Preserve the job of each passage: narrative movement, a character's hesitation, an explanation, or supporting evidence. Short dialogue can still have full punctuation. A text-message exchange quoted inside a novel may keep its chat conventions.

For fiction, check speaker changes, speech tags, interruptions, and trailing-off against the intended scene. Do not replace an interrupted sentence with a completed one or turn a quiet pause into an emphatic exclamation. Preserve deliberate stylistic departures when they are part of the author's voice.

For nonfiction, retain reasoning, examples, qualifications, and references. Break dense passages where readers need a new step in the argument, without reducing every paragraph to a social caption. Use the [documentation guide](documentation.md) for instructional sections and the [blog and essay guide](blogs.md) for developed arguments.

For a publication-ready manuscript, check the specified quotation style, dash and ellipsis treatment, notes, and consistent chapter formatting. Distinguish text editing from layout: vertical type, line-breaking, hanging punctuation, and font-specific glyph placement require inspection in the actual publishing format. Do not claim to have verified print layout from Markdown.
