# Blogs and essays

Find what makes this piece worth reading: an experience, question, argument, explanation, or observation the author actually has. Build around that material. A personal essay can discover its point as it goes; a practical technical article should make its topic and intended reader clear early.

## Develop the thought

Choose details that explain the author's judgment. In a technical article, show the problem or surprising result before tracing the explanation. In a reflective piece, preserve the events and uncertainty that lead to the thought. When the author has no settled conclusion, an honest unresolved ending can fit.

Use transitions to move from what the reader knows to the next thing they need. Keep repetition that revisits a concept with a new example or consequence. Cut restatements that merely announce how significant or complex the topic is. Headings should identify the section's actual question or subject; a short essay may need none.

Write an opening the material earns. It might be a direct claim, a real scene, or a specific problem. Preserve useful voice, humor, and rhythm without manufacturing a personal story or forcing every article through the same hook and conclusion.

An explanatory article can be longer because it teaches. Keep the example, intermediate reasoning, and limits needed for understanding. Shorten where the reader gains nothing, not wherever a sentence exceeds an arbitrary length.

## Original example

Notes: the author moved image decoding out of a loop; ten repeated images previously caused ten decodes; in a local test rendering fell from 120 ms to 80 ms; distinct images are untested.

台灣版：

「同一張圖片出現十次，就會解碼十次。我把解碼移到迴圈外之後，這個本機測試的繪圖時間從 120 ms 降到 80 ms。不過這次用的是重複圖片，換成十張不同的圖片會怎樣，還沒測。」

The observed result carries the story. An initial mistaken assumption, a frustrating afternoon, or a moment of discovery would need to come from the author before it belongs in the draft.

## Completion check

Check that the reader can follow how the author gets from the opening to the ending. Every important claim needs its supporting material or an honest qualification. Preserve the writer's stance, including uncertainty and disagreement. The ending should complete the piece's purpose; an essay need not end with advice, and a tutorial need not end with a life lesson.
