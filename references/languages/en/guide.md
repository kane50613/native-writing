# English

Read [punctuation by context](punctuation.md) alongside this guide. Consult [sources](sources.md) when checking the evidence.

Write in complete, connected thoughts. For ordinary messages and explanations, use the vocabulary the reader already uses, contractions where they fit, and verbs that tell us what happened. An expert audience can handle precise terminology; a new reader may need a short explanation at first use.

## Choices that matter

**Put the relationship into the sentence.** A pile of labels makes the reader reconstruct the argument. Name what depends on what, what changed, or why someone chose an approach. Keep a longer sentence when it makes that relationship easier to follow.

**Make the degree of certainty legible.** Attach uncertainty to the particular claim it qualifies. A result observed in one test stays a result from one test. A promise stays distinct from a plan.

**Let the writer have a voice.** A conversational aside can carry useful context. A courteous request can be direct and still courteous. Preserve distinctive phrasing when it works; polishing everything into the same neutral tone loses information about the speaker.

**End where the message ends.** A work update usually ends with the next action or remaining issue. An explanation may end with the last piece of evidence. A personal post can end with the writer's reaction. Choose the ending the content supports.

## Examples

These are original editing exercises, not community quotations. Each rewrite uses only the facts in its input.

### Explain an awkward label

Input: “The implementation introduces a retry-boundary ownership model: the caller decides whether a failed request should be retried.”

Rewrite: “The caller decides whether to retry a failed request.”

The second clause already supplies the entire meaning of the label. If the label were an established API name that readers needed to find, retain it.

### Keep the evidence and its limit

Input: “The cache delivered a significant performance enhancement, reducing startup from 800 ms to 500 ms in one local test, although its production impact has yet to be validated.”

Rewrite: “The cache cut startup from 800 ms to 500 ms in one local test. We haven't tested it in production yet.”

### Preserve a real contrast

Input: “The preview updates immediately, but saving still takes about two seconds.”

Rewrite: unchanged.

The contrast tells the reader that two operations behave differently. The sentence is already clear.

### Keep courtesy and the reason for asking

Input: “I wanted to reach out to ask whether you might be able to send the revised invoice by Friday, as we need it to close this month's accounts.”

Rewrite: “Could you send the revised invoice by Friday? We need it to close this month's accounts.”

### Keep the human detail

Input: “I bought it for the battery life. The keyboard is weirdly loud, though. My partner can hear it from the next room.”

Rewrite: unchanged.

The detail explains the complaint. Turning it into a generic assessment of the typing experience would make it less useful.
